# END-OF-SEASON

## 125. General Performance Dimensions for Athlete Evaluation

Discipline: The ability to control impulses, emotions, desires, and behavior. It includes avoiding instant gratification and refusing to become satisfied with past success.

Mental Toughness: The degree to which a player can cope with the demands of competition, training, and the athletic lifestyle. It involves remaining determined, focused, confident, resilient, and in control under pressure throughout the year.

Internal Standards: The degree to which a player sets high personal standards instead of relying on the coach, captain, or others to establish them.

Coachability: The ability to receive coaching with a positive attitude, apply feedback, and make adjustments that improve individual and team performance.

Positive Practice Transfer: The ability to transfer the positive attitude, improved skills, and team chemistry demonstrated in practice into real competition.

Motivation: The general desire, passion, and willingness to contribute as an important member of the team.

Selflessness: Putting the needs of others ahead of personal recognition and not expecting something in return for every contribution.

Consistency: Maintaining a high level of attitude, effort, and performance regardless of the opponent, day, or circumstances.

Dependability: Being reliable and willing to invest the necessary time and effort to help teammates complete important tasks.

Family Mentality: Treating the team like a family in which mutual trust and respect are understood and valued.
## 126. Additional Athlete Evaluation Dimensions

Leadership: The ability to inspire and motivate others while holding oneself and teammates accountable constructively. Leaders are willing to speak up and take the initiative for positive change.

Fear of Failure: Excessive self-consciousness about mistakes or failing to achieve a high level of performance. The player may react very negatively to unsuccessful outcomes and lose composure under pressure.

Task Persistence: The ability to stay committed and persevere despite distractions, physical or emotional discomfort, or a lack of immediate success.

Personality: The combination of emotional, attitudinal, and behavioral response patterns that characterize an individual. Personality may positively or negatively influence the team environment.

Team Chemistry: Being fully committed to the team’s goals, rules, and overall success. This quality includes many other performance dimensions and contributes to cohesion and morale.
## 127. Continuous Evaluation and Improvement

Success is never final, and failure is never fatal.

Learning never ends, and knowledge is power.

At the end of each year, the entire organization should undergo an extensive evaluation.

Identify what was done well and where performance fell short of expectations.

The most important part of the process is deciding how the organization will continue to innovate, learn, and improve.
## 128. Recognize What Is Working

Recognize and celebrate the values and behaviors that create the foundation for success.

Do not focus only on winning.
## 129. Award Banquets and End-of-Season Rituals

An end-of-season ceremony serves as a bridge between the past and the future.

It is important for building a durable culture of excellence.
## 130. Guidelines for Recognition Speeches

Personalize comments.

Highlight unique aspects of each athlete’s personality.

Practice your delivery and timing.

Set a time limit for each recognition and stick to it.

Do not force humor.

When using humor, avoid inside jokes that exclude others.

Keep comments positive.

Avoid sarcasm and negative remarks.

Write comments in advance.

Emphasize positive qualities and contributions.

End-of-season recognition can also be used to identify and encourage potential leaders for the following season.
## 131. Focus on Strengths

Why not focus on strengths instead of weaknesses?

Use the best tools available.

Apply strengths in different ways and to different degrees depending on the situation.

Adaptability is a defining characteristic of wise strength application.

Three strength-spotting strategies are:

1. Day-to-day strength spotting.
2. Listening for strengths.
3. Strength-based interviewing.
## 132. Day-to-Day Strength Spotting

Ask another person to observe you, or record yourself on video.

Look for patterns.

Moments of strength may be revealed through:

* Heightened energy.
* Positive and desired responses from athletes or coaches.
* Successful and timely completion of tasks.
## 133. Listening for Strengths

Pay attention to how you sound when speaking about your work.

Do you speak clearly and confidently?

Do you sound enthusiastic?

Do you provide detailed examples?

Do you sound calm yet energetic?

Are you optimistic and forward-looking?

Does the conversation flow naturally?

These vocal patterns can reveal strengths.

Listen carefully to yourself and invite others to provide feedback.
## 134. Strength-Based Interviewing

Ask yourself:

What was your best day of coaching this season, and why?

Describe a specific situation in which you were at your best as a coach.

What aspect of coaching energizes you most?
## 135. Coaching Strengths and Positive Psychology

A strengths-based approach, drawn from positive psychology, focuses on virtues and character strengths associated with living a meaningful, engaged, and happy life.

| Virtue               | Sample Strengths                             |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Wisdom and Knowledge | Creativity, curiosity, open-mindedness       |
| Courage              | Bravery, persistence                         |
| Humanity             | Kindness, love, social intelligence, empathy |
| Justice              | Fairness, leadership, teamwork               |
| Temperance           | Forgiveness, modesty, prudence               |
| Transcendence        | Gratitude, hope, humor                       |
## 136. Benefits of Virtues

Virtues help coaches maintain the positive outlook and high energy needed to lead athletes and teams.

Coaches and athletes who demonstrate positive virtues are more likely to maintain well-being, self-esteem, vitality, and lower levels of stress.
## 137. Developing Coaching Resilience

Recognizing strengths helps build coaching confidence and mental toughness.

The ability to overcome failures and emotionally difficult situations is a key characteristic of a resilient coach.

Mastering one area allows the coach to direct attention toward other areas requiring development.

Developing strengths and resilience requires self-confidence, sometimes referred to as coaching efficacy.
## 138. Dimensions of Coaching Efficacy

1. Game Strategy Efficacy: Confidence in decision-making and the ability to lead athletes and teams during competition.
2. Character-Building Efficacy: Belief in the ability to teach values, integrity, morality, and respect.
3. Physical Conditioning Efficacy: The ability to design appropriate strength and conditioning programs.
4. Motivational Efficacy: The ability to help athletes regulate emotions, maintain focus, and develop positive attitudes.
5. Teaching Efficacy: The ability to teach the sport clearly and effectively.
6. Academic Efficacy: In school-based programs, the ability to support student-athletes in succeeding academically.

Recognizing and developing strengths can help coaches build confidence, resilience, and mental toughness.
## 139. Mental Toughness Model

1. Control: The ability to remain focused and disciplined while managing multiple and sometimes competing demands. Prioritize responsibilities, delegate when possible, and make time to prepare practice and competition plans.

2. Challenge: The ability to view constant challenges and changes as opportunities for growth and development.

3. Commitment: The persistent pursuit of goals despite difficulties. Plan backward and execute forward.

4. Confidence: The ability to maintain self-belief despite setbacks.
## 140. Off-Season — Closing Performance Gaps

Recognize and build on strengths while also identifying weaknesses and areas for growth.

Create a clear plan to address them.

The off-season is an ideal time for coach development and learning more about effective coaching.
