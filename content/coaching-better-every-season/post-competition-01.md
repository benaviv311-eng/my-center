# POST-COMPETITION

## 119. After a Loss

Do not force athletes into an extensive debrief immediately after a loss.

It can wait until the next day.

Performance corrections may not be absorbed while emotions are still high.

Waiting gives everyone greater clarity and perspective.
## 120. After a Win

A win is a good opportunity to teach humility.

It is also an appropriate time to identify and address performance weaknesses.

When the team is winning and performing confidently, it can still search for ways to improve.
## 121. Match Reflection

The coach should reflect on the match later, but no later than one day afterward.

Look beyond the final result.

Analyze what actually happened and how well the team performed.
## 122. One-Page Competition Reflection Card

A brief one-page competition reflection card can provide significant value.

Basic Match Information:

* Result.
* Location.
* Opponent.
* Referee.

Quality of Performance:

* Competitiveness.
* Key performance indicators.
* Teamwork.
* Positivity.
* Positioning.
* Readiness.

Summary:

* Player of the match.
* Areas for improvement.
* Key points.
* Point or moment of the match.
* Strategies for facing the same or a similar opponent in the future.
## 123. Special Notes for the Next Competition

After the match, record specific notes about how to succeed the next time against the same or a similar opponent.

The most practical lesson about coaching a match is that there should not be too much for the coach to do.

If the coach and team have prepared properly, the coach should be able to sit back and listen to the match as it unfolds.

Respond only with an occasional corrective or congratulatory comment when necessary.
## 124. Implement an Evaluation System

A coach’s job is not over when the season ends. It simply changes.

The end of the season is the time to collect information and make informed decisions about improvement.

Identify what worked well and what needs to change.

Design a formal evaluation system to improve your own performance as a coach.

Identify what should be evaluated.

Collect evidence from multiple sources, including athletes, coaches, and administrators.

Make sure the evaluation tools are practical.

Decide how the results will be communicated and used.

A meaningful program evaluation should answer four questions:

1. How well did we model our purpose and core values?
2. How well did we build a culture of trust and cohesion?
3. How well did we develop athletes through a quality training season?
4. How well did we prepare athletes to perform at their peak in competition?
